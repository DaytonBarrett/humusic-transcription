§1

Index

What the machine does

  1. 01

    Capture

    Record straight from the microphone with start and stop under your control. Echo cancellation, noise suppression and auto-gain are held off deliberately — each one distorts the periodicity that pitch detection depends on.

  2. 02

    Detection

    The YIN autocorrelation estimator resolves fundamental frequency and rejects the octave errors that spectral peak-picking is prone to. Deviation is reported live, in cents, while you play.

  3. 03

    Quantisation

    Audio is divided against a grid of twelfths of a beat derived from the tempo you set, so rhythm is measured rather than guessed. Twelve divides by both four and three, which is what lets a sixteenth and a triplet be measured exactly on the same grid. Each beat is then read as straight or as a triplet according to where the notes in it actually fell.

  4. 04

    Engraving

    Repeated units are consolidated into idiomatic durations — dotted halves, quarters, tied values across barlines — then set with beams, rests and accidentals in the way a copyist would write them. The clef is yours to set — treble by default, bass when the line calls for it, or read automatically from the range you played so a bass line lands on a bass staff rather than strung below a treble one on ledger lines.

  5. 05

    Playback

    Hear the transcription rather than the recording — as a piano, a plucked guitar, a saxophone or a plain synth. What sounds is the engraved model, so a wrong note is audible as a wrong note instead of having to be read off the staff. Every voice is synthesised on the spot; nothing is downloaded, and the page still runs with no network at all.

  6. 06

    Export

    One file, MusicXML 4.0, built from the same model that drew the page. The exported score and the printed score cannot disagree.

  7. 07

    Locality

    Every stage runs on your own device. No recording is uploaded, and no account stands between you and the score.

§2

Method

Three movements

I

Set the tempo

Give the transcriber a beats-per-minute figure. Everything downstream — the analysis grid, the bar lines, the note values — is measured against it, so this single number decides how your rhythm is read. Leave the metronome on and it counts that tempo while you record, so the pulse you play against is the one the grid is measuring.

II

Play the line

Press record and perform one voice at a time: a melody, a bass line, a hummed idea. The pitch monitor shows what is being heard, in cents, as it is heard.

III

Take the file

Stop, and the score is engraved on the page. Export MusicXML and open it in the notation software you already use — nothing is locked in.

§3

Origin

Why it exists

Notation is a skill that takes years to learn, and good ideas disappear in the gap.

humusic began as a frustration: musical ideas vanish because most people cannot write them down fast enough to keep them. Whether you have decades of training or only ever hum in the shower, the thing you just played deserves to exist on paper.

The transcriber is the bridge between hearing music and writing it — professional-quality notation, without the years of study that usually stand in front of it.

Privacy policy

Privacy policy

Last Updated: September 1, 2026

The short version: humusic has no accounts, no cookies, no analytics and no tracking of any kind. Your recordings and the scores made from them never leave your device — there is no server for them to be sent to. The only personal information that ever reaches me is what you choose to put in an email and send yourself.

Who this covers: This policy applies to the humusic website at humusic.org, the web transcriber hosted there, and the humusic app for iOS. In this policy "I" and "me" mean Dayton Barrett, the sole developer of humusic, who is the data controller for anything described below.

Audio and recordings: Audio captured by the transcriber is recorded, analysed and discarded entirely on your own device. It is never uploaded, never stored on any server, and never accessible to me or to anyone else. The transcriber performs its pitch detection and notation in your browser or in the app itself. The iOS app ships with no network access at all — its build fails if any file in it so much as references a remote address.

Scores you save: The library keeps your saved notation and its tempo in your browser's local storage, on your own device. It never holds the audio. Nothing in the library is transmitted anywhere, and I cannot see it. Clearing your browser's site data, or deleting the app, deletes it permanently — there is no copy elsewhere and no way for me to restore it.

Microphone: The transcriber asks for microphone access solely to hear the line you are transcribing, and only while you are recording. Your device will ask your permission before this happens, and you can withdraw it at any time in your browser or system settings.

No accounts, cookies or analytics: There is no sign-up and no account. The site sets no cookies and uses no similar tracking technology. There is no analytics package, no advertising network, no third-party script and no tracking pixel on any page — neither the website nor the app contains one.

The enquiry form: The contact form on this site does not submit anything to a server. It assembles what you typed into a message and opens it in your own email program, already addressed to me. Nothing is sent until you review it and press send yourself, and if you close it instead, what you typed simply goes nowhere.

If you email me: When you do send that message, or write to me directly, I receive an ordinary email containing whatever you chose to include — typically your name, your email address, and what you wanted to say. I use it only to reply to you and to keep track of the conversation. I do not add you to a mailing list, and I do not share, sell or disclose your correspondence to anyone, except where the law requires it. Email reaches me through Google's Gmail service, which processes it under its own privacy policy. I keep correspondence for as long as it is useful to the matter you wrote about, and you can ask me to delete it at any time.

Website hosting and server logs: humusic.org is a set of static files served by GitHub Pages. Like every web host, GitHub's servers record ordinary request information such as IP address and browser type in order to deliver pages and guard against abuse. Those logs belong to GitHub and are handled under GitHub's Privacy Statement. I do not receive them, have no access to them, and collect nothing of my own about your visit.

The iOS app: The humusic iOS app collects no data whatsoever. It has no analytics, no accounts, and no code that sends anything anywhere. It makes no network requests of any kind, and works fully with the device in aeroplane mode, which is the plainest demonstration of the point. When you export a score, the file is handed to your device's own share sheet, and where it goes from there is entirely your choice and governed by whichever app you send it to.

Children's privacy: humusic is not directed at children under 16, and I do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone under 16. Since the software collects no personal information from anyone, there is nothing to collect from a child either; if you believe a child has sent me an email that ought to be deleted, write to me and I will delete it.

Your rights: Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, delete, or restrict the use of personal information held about you, and to complain to a data protection authority. In practice there is very little for those rights to reach, because I hold nothing about you unless you have written to me. If you have, you can ask me to send you a copy of that correspondence or to delete it, and I will do so. There is no charge, and I do not discriminate against anyone for asking. I do not sell or share personal information, and never have.

Security: The strongest protection here is structural rather than procedural: data that is never collected cannot be breached, and the recordings and scores that matter most stay on your own device throughout. The site is served over HTTPS. No method of transmission over the internet is completely secure, so nothing can be guaranteed absolutely.

Changes to this policy: If humusic ever begins collecting anything — it does not today, and there are no plans for it to — this policy will be updated before that change ships, and the date above will change with it. Material changes will be noted here rather than made quietly.
